# No free Funko API exists; combine three sources instead

There is no official or community API, free or paid, that cleanly does "search a Funko Pop by name and get its image" for a hobby project in October 2026. Funko has never published a developer API and its Terms forbid building apps that talk to its services; hobbyDB (which runs Pop Price Guide, the de-facto catalog of ~27,000 Pops) sells API access only after project screening and a **setup fee of at least $1,200**; and every generic marketplace API that could stand in has either shut down (Amazon PA-API 5, Bing Image Search, Google Custom Search for new users) or needs affiliate sales you do not have. What does work for a one-person, LAN-only app in Portugal is a layered approach: **UPCitemdb's free, no-signup barcode lookup (100 requests/day)** for scanned boxes, **eBay's Browse API (free developer account, 5,000 calls/day, app token only)** for name search and seller photos, and the **MIT-licensed kennymkchan/funko-pop-data dump (23,940 rows, frozen January 2021)** as a one-time seed of names, series and image URLs. Funko's own storefront exposes an undocumented Salesforce Commerce Cloud JSON endpoint (`Product-Variation?pid=`) that returns the box number, license, series, vaulted flag and 800 px official renders for a 29,062-item sitemap, and it is by far the richest data; but robots.txt and the Terms explicitly disallow it, so it belongs in the "knowingly out of bounds, low enforcement risk at home" column rather than the recommended path. The legal picture is uniform across all sources: product photos are Funko's copyright wherever they are hosted, no provider grants a personal-use image-storage exception, and a private single-user server is the lowest-risk scenario that exists but is not blessed by anyone's terms.

## Funko publishes no API, but its storefront leaks the best catalog

Funko has never shipped a developer program. The 2019 app relaunch (which absorbed Stashpedia), the November 2023 switch to eBay-derived values, and the May 2025 v8.0.0 rebuild all added consumer features only, and the current App Store listing advertises "over 27,000 official Funko collectibles" with no API or data-partner statement ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/funko/id1286964746); [Funko blog, 2025-05-16](https://funko.com/funko-blog-home/new-app-features-are-here-level-up-your-funko-experience.html)). The Terms & Conditions go further than silence: §4.1 bars users from "Develop[ing] or us[ing] any applications that interact with our Services without our prior written consent", from reverse engineering, and from data mining "except in accordance with instructions contained in our robots.txt file" ([Funko Terms & Conditions](https://funko.com/terms-and-conditions/terms-conditions-v1.html)).

Technically, though, funko.com is an ordinary Salesforce Commerce Cloud (SFRA) store, and its standard controllers answer plain HTTP. Live probes on 2026-10-22 showed `https://funko.com/on/demandware.store/Sites-FunkoUS-Site/en_US/Product-Variation?pid={itemNumber}` returning ~10–20 KB of JSON with **109 product keys**, including `boxNumber` (the Pop! number on the box), `license`, `sublicense`, `series`, `fandom`, `exclusivity`, `vaulted`, `ebayVaultedValue`, and an `images` object with 150/346/800 px renders; it worked with browser, curl and python-requests user agents ([Product-Variation?pid=93170](https://funko.com/on/demandware.store/Sites-FunkoUS-Site/en_US/Product-Variation?pid=93170)). A sibling `Search-UpdateGrid?q=...&vault=true&sz=200` endpoint returns keyword search results as HTML tiles with `data-pid` attributes, including vaulted (retired) items, and `csitemap_product.xml` enumerates **29,062 product URLs** ([Search-UpdateGrid example](https://funko.com/on/demandware.store/Sites-FunkoUS-Site/en_US/Search-UpdateGrid?q=batman&start=0&sz=12); [csitemap_product.xml](https://funko.com/csitemap_product.xml)). The official renders are served from Salesforce's Dynamic Imaging Service with `?sw=&sh=` size parameters and no referer check at fetch time ([example 800 px image](https://funko.com/dw/image/v2/BGTS_PRD/on/demandware.static/-/Sites-funko-master-catalog/default/dw26d2241e/images/funko/upload/1/93170_DC_NWBATADV_Batman_POP_render_v1-Glam-WEB.png?sw=800&sh=800)). The same endpoint works against `Sites-FunkoEU-Site/en_GB`, which is where funkoeurope.com now redirects.

Two things keep this from being the recommendation. First, **robots.txt disallows every one of these paths** (`/on/demandware.store/*`, `*Product-Variation`, `*?q=`, `*?sz=`, `*start=`, `*cgid`), which means the Terms' robots.txt carve-out does not apply and §4.1 is squarely breached ([funko.com/robots.txt](https://funko.com/robots.txt)). Second, Funko runs Cloudflare and says it continuously tunes bot rules on IP traffic and hit frequency; full product HTML pages already return 403 to non-browser clients while the JSON controllers happen to return 200, a gap that can close without notice ([Digital Commerce 360, 2024-07-03](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2024/07/03/funko-dtc-sales-combat-bots/)). The endpoint also carries no UPC/EAN, so it cannot serve a barcode-scan flow on its own. Funko's affiliate program (CJ Affiliate in the US, Awin for the UK) is the only sanctioned data channel, and even if it ships a product feed, affiliate feed licences cover promoting Funko's links, not building a catalog ([FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/funko-us-affiliate-program/)).

## hobbyDB owns the real catalog and prices it out of reach

Pop Price Guide, the catalog Funko itself partnered with for "over 27,000 Funko verified collectibles", is a hobbyDB brand; poppriceguide.com now 301-redirects to hobbydb.com ([AppFollow listing of Funko app](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/funko/1286964746?country=br); [Funko investor press release 2019](https://investor.funko.com/news-and-events/press-releases/Press-Releases/2019/Funko-Unveils-New-Mobile-Application-with-Fan-Favorite-Capabilities/default.aspx)). hobbyDB describes its architecture as "fully based on Restful APIs" with items, values, subjects and listings all accessible, but access requires submitting a project overview with monetization and team details, and "unless the project brings significant traffic to hobbyDB" there is **"a one-off setup fee of at least $1,200"** plus unpublished monthly charges ([hobbyDB Help: Access to the hobbyDB API](https://help.hobbydb.com/support/solutions/articles/36000265069-access-to-the-hobbydb-api)). For a one-person LAN app this is a non-option.

The unofficial route is worse than it looks. A commercial Apify actor once advertised "HTTP-only via hobbyDB's public JSON API", but on 2026-10-22 it returned 404 and an Apify store search for "hobbydb" found zero actors, consistent with hobbyDB enforcing its terms ([Apify store search](https://api.apify.com/v2/store?search=hobbydb&limit=10)). Direct probes of `hobbydb.com/api/catalog_items` returned **HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare interactive challenge** even with a browser user agent ([hobbydb.com/api/catalog_items probe](https://www.hobbydb.com/api/catalog_items?page%5Bsize%5D=1)). And hobbyDB's Terms are the strictest of any source surveyed: no "robots, spiders, scripts ... or any manual or automatic device" without written consent, robots.txt does not count as consent, no caching or archiving of content, an explicit text-and-data-mining opt-out under Article 4 of the EU DSM Directive (which binds a user in Portugal), and a ban on using content "for the development of any software program, model, algorithm, or generative AI tool" ([hobbyDB Terms & Conditions](https://help.hobbydb.com/support/solutions/articles/36000264473-terms-conditions)). PriceCharting is the only other documented catalog-style API with Funko coverage; it is a paid "premium feature" whose docs sit behind a Cloudflare JavaScript challenge and whose price no public source states ([PriceCharting API documentation](https://www.pricecharting.com/api-documentation)).

## UPCitemdb and eBay Browse are the free, legitimate working pair

Two generic APIs are self-serve, free at hobby volumes, usable from Portugal, and have real Funko coverage. **UPCitemdb** needs no signup on its `/prod/trial` path and allows **100 combined requests per day**; a live lookup of UPC 889698624008 returned the title "Funko Pop! Television Stranger Things #1250 Eddie Munson Stickered", brand Funko, and two retailer stock photos hotlinked from Walmart's and Target's CDNs with size parameters in the query string ([UPCitemdb trial lookup](https://api.upcitemdb.com/prod/trial/lookup?upc=889698624008); [devs.upcitemdb.com](https://devs.upcitemdb.com/)). A trial search for "funko pop" reported **95,764 matching items** with `upc`, `ean`, `title`, `brand`, `model`, `category` and `images[]` fields, though a second search probe in the same day returned 404, so name search on the free path is unreliable and UPC lookup is the dependable part ([UPCitemdb trial search](https://api.upcitemdb.com/prod/trial/search?s=funko+pop&match_mode=0&type=product)). Every Funko UPC observed shares the GS1 prefix **889698**, a cheap "is this a Funko item" filter. Expect strong coverage for mass-retail Pops and weak coverage for convention and EU-only exclusives, because the data is retailer listings.

**eBay Browse API** is the other half. New developer accounts get a free tier of **5,000 API calls per day**, every Browse method needs only an application token from the client-credentials flow (no user login), `item_summary/search` accepts free text or a `gtin` parameter, and `getItem` returns the full picture set ([eBay API call limits](https://developer.ebay.com/develop/api/buy/api_call_limits); [Browse API overview](https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/buy/browse/overview.html)). Its strength is that every Pop ever made is listed somewhere with photos; its weakness is noise, since one Pop maps to many listings and Funko numbering is "a pervasive issue" even in retailer data ([Yakface forums](https://yakfaceforums.com/main/?p=38154)). Use `EBAY_US` as the marketplace for coverage (the `EBAY_PT` enum is labelled "Reserved for future use" in one of eBay's own type docs) and fall back to `EBAY_ES`/`EBAY_DE`/`EBAY_GB` for European exclusives ([Post-Order MarketplaceIdEnum](https://developer.ebay.com/DevZone/post-order/types/MarketplaceIdEnum.html)). Picture URLs encode size in a suffix, and swapping it to `s-l1600` yields the 1600 px rendition where the seller uploaded one ([eBay community](https://community.ebay.com/t5/Report-eBay-Technical-Issues/Pictures-not-supersizing-when-under-800-px/td-p/33257308/jump-to/first-unread-message)). Note that the Finding API that most older tutorials use was decommissioned on 2025-02-05 and the API licence was rewritten on 2025-09-05 ([eBay API deprecation page](https://developer.ebay.com/docs/api-deprecation); [EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/30a9)).

The alternatives are closed or dead. Amazon PA-API 5 now returns 403 "deprecated", and its successor Creators API requires at least **10 qualifying affiliate sales in the past 30 days** ([Register for Creators API](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/onboarding/register-for-creators-api)). Google's Custom Search JSON API "is closed to new customers" and ends 2027-01-01 ([Custom Search JSON API overview](https://developers.google.com/custom-search/v1/overview)); Bing Search APIs were retired on 2025-08-11 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/15/bing_search_apis_retired/)). Walmart needs Impact-network publisher approval, Best Buy is US-centric and forbids non-temporary caching ([Best Buy API documentation](https://bestbuyapis.github.io/api-documentation/)). Paid barcode APIs (Barcode Lookup from $99/month, Go-UPC from $74.95/month, EAN-Search from 9 EUR/month but without images) add nothing a hobbyist needs ([barcodelookup.com/api](https://www.barcodelookup.com/api); [go-upc.com/plans/api](https://go-upc.com/plans/api); [EAN-Search API](https://www.ean-search.org/ean-database-api.html)). Open Products Facts, Wikimedia Commons and Wikidata are fully open but effectively empty for Funko: **4 products**, no per-figure photos, and 1 Wikidata item respectively ([OPF search](https://world.openproductsfacts.org/cgi/search.pl?search_terms=funko&search_simple=1&action=process&json=1&page_size=10&fields=code,product_name,image_url); [Commons categorymembers](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&list=categorymembers&cmtitle=Category:Funko&cmlimit=50&format=json)). SerpApi's 250 free Google Images searches a month can serve as a manual last-resort picker, but it is a scraper with only a US "legal shield" ([SerpApi Google Images](https://serpapi.com/images-results)).

## One open dataset seeds names and series, but not Pop numbers

GitHub holds 1,939 "funko" repositories, but almost all are student CRUD projects or scrapers that publish no data. The single sizeable open dataset is **kennymkchan/funko-pop-data**: MIT-licensed, **23,940 entries** with `handle`, `title`, `imageName` and a `series` array (for example `["Pop! Vinyl","Pop! Zodiac"]`), also mirrored to a public Google Sheet ([github.com/kennymkchan/funko-pop-data](https://github.com/kennymkchan/funko-pop-data)). Its limits are hard ones: the README says "Last Updated January 3, 2021", only 179 titles contain a "#number" token so there is **no Pop number field**, there is no UPC, and the author now marks it deprecated in favour of popiq.dev, which on 2026-10-22 returned a Vercel "DEPLOYMENT_DISABLED" page ([popiq.dev](https://popiq.dev/)). Against a collector count of **27,045 releases as of September 2026**, it is roughly five years and 3,000 figures behind ([japan-figure.com](https://japan-figure.com/blogs/news/how-many-funko-pops-are-there)). All 23,940 image URLs point at `images.hobbydb.com`, and a sample still served `200 image/jpeg` without a Cloudflare challenge, so the MIT licence covers the JSON but not the pictures, which remain hobbyDB-hosted and Funko-copyrighted ([raw funko_pop.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kennymkchan/funko-pop-data/master/funko_pop.json)).

Nothing else fills the gap. The Funko Fandom wiki offers a free MediaWiki API under CC-BY-SA but holds only **1,761 articles** ([funko.fandom.com/api.php siteinfo](https://funko.fandom.com/api.php?action=query&meta=siteinfo&siprop=statistics|general|rightsinfo&format=json)). A Kaggle "Funko Pop Dataset" of items "listed on the official shop" exists but its schema and licence sit behind a reCAPTCHA ([kaggle.com/datasets/victorsoeiro/funko-pop-dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/victorsoeiro/funko-pop-dataset)). Hugging Face "funko" datasets are small image folders or synthetic sets for machine learning ([HF datasets API](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets?search=funko&limit=50)). No open dataset anywhere maps UPC to Pop number to series; that mapping lives only inside closed apps (hobbyDB, PopArchive, Coleka, iCollect) and generic UPC databases.

## Recommended order of sources and the legal caveats

| Priority | Source | Cost and limit | What it gives | Terms status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | UPCitemdb `/prod/trial/lookup` | Free, no key, 100/day | Title with series/number, brand, retailer photos, by UPC | Licence for "Customer's operations"; eBay/Amazon offer data "cannot redistribute"; silent on image caching ([upcitemdb.com/terms](https://www.upcitemdb.com/terms)) |
| 2 | eBay Browse API | Free dev account, 5,000/day | Name and GTIN search, seller photos, `getItem` full images | Display "through your Application" only; eBay Content must be visually isolated from non-eBay content; privacy-policy link required; current 2025 licence text unverified ([eBay API License](https://developer.ebay.com/products/license)) |
| 3 | kennymkchan/funko-pop-data | Free, one-time download | 23,940 names, series, hobbyDB image URLs | MIT for data; images are hobbyDB-hosted, hobbyDB forbids copying ([hobbyDB T&C](https://help.hobbydb.com/support/solutions/articles/36000264473-terms-conditions)) |
| Out of bounds | funko.com `Product-Variation` / `Search-UpdateGrid` | Free, undocumented, unthrottled as far as tested | Box number, license, series, vaulted flag, 800 px official renders | Disallowed by robots.txt and T&C §4.1; Cloudflare may close it any day |
| Avoid | hobbyDB undocumented JSON | Behind Cloudflare challenge | Full catalog and prices | Explicit scripts/caching/TDM/software-development ban |
| Avoid | Amazon, Google CSE, Bing, Walmart, Best Buy | Closed, dead or gated | — | — |

Start with UPCitemdb for anything you can scan, fall back to eBay Browse for name queries and for items UPCitemdb misses (exclusives, pre-2015 Pops), and use the kennymkchan dump only to pre-fill an autocomplete of names and series so manual entry is fast. The practical design, borrowed from Grocy's pluggable `STOCK_BARCODE_LOOKUP_PLUGIN` pattern, is an `ImageLookupProvider` chain where each provider is one class and storage policy is a per-provider setting ([Grocy README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/grocy/grocy@master/README.md)). A few hundred Pops fit inside a single day's combined quota.

On the legal side, three facts hold regardless of source. Box art and figure renders are Funko's (and its licensors') copyrighted artwork; every third-party source is re-hosting them. **No provider surveyed grants a personal-use exception for storing images**: Amazon allows image links for 24 hours only, Best Buy allows caching "on a temporary basis" with links expiring in seven days, eBay frames its licence around display through your app rather than archival, and UPCitemdb, Go-UPC and Barcode Lookup are silent ([Amazon Associates Program Policies](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/policies); [Best Buy API documentation](https://bestbuyapis.github.io/api-documentation/)). The terms-respecting design is to store the source URL, provider and item ID and re-fetch with a short cache; the design most hobby apps actually use is "download one thumbnail per owned figure to disk", and a non-public LAN server with one user is the lowest-risk version of that there is. For a Portuguese user the one source-specific sharp edge is hobbyDB's EU DSM Article 4 text-and-data-mining opt-out, which is written precisely to remove the research-style mining defence that EU law otherwise offers. Funko's Terms say use of its sites is "currently authorized only in the United States and Canada" under Washington law, which is one more reason the funko.com endpoints stay in the out-of-bounds column even though they are the best data ([Funko Terms & Conditions](https://funko.com/terms-and-conditions/terms-conditions-v1.html)). The only clean route to Funko's own renders is written permission, via feedback@funko.com.

## Conclusion

The market has consolidated the Funko catalog into two walled gardens, Funko's Salesforce storefront and hobbyDB's Pop Price Guide, while the generic APIs hobbyists used to lean on (Amazon PA-API, eBay Finding, Bing, Google CSE) were all switched off or gated between February 2025 and spring 2026. The result is that a "search by name, get the image" feature in 2026 is not a single integration but a routing problem: barcodes to UPCitemdb, names to eBay, and a frozen 2021 dump for autocomplete, with the user picking the right thumbnail from a short list. That is a weekend of work, not a research project, and it degrades gracefully if any one provider changes.

The deeper implication is that Pop numbers and lines, the fields a collector cares about most, are not in any legitimately accessible machine-readable source; they arrive only as text inside retailer and seller titles, or inside the forbidden funko.com JSON. Design the data model so `boxNumber` and `series` are user-editable fields that lookups merely suggest, and keep provider adapters thin enough that a future sanctioned feed (a Funko affiliate catalog, a cheaper hobbyDB tier, a revived popiq.dev) can slot in without touching the rest of the app.
